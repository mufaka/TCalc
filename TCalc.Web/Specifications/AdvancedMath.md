# Advanced Probability Models
These concepts deal with systems that evolve over time or involve multiple interdependent variables. 

- Bayesian Statistics: A framework where you update the probability of a hypothesis (posterior) as you gain more evidence, starting from an initial belief (prior).
- Markov Chains: Sequential models where the probability of the next state depends only on the current state, not the entire history.
- Stochastic Processes: Systems that evolve randomly over time, such as Brownian Motion (used in finance) or Poisson Processes for modeling event arrivals.
- Information Theory (Entropy): Measuring the amount of uncertainty or "surprise" in a probability distribution. 

# Inference and Estimation
These techniques are used to pull deep insights from sample data to describe a whole population. 

- Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE): A method for estimating the parameters of a distribution by finding the values that make the observed data most likely.
- The Central Limit Theorem (CLT): The bedrock of modern statistics, stating that the sum or average of many independent variables will follow a normal distribution, regardless of the original data's shape.
- Hypothesis Testing & P-Values: Rigorous ways to determine if an observed effect is statistically significant or just a result of random noise.
- Non-Parametric Statistics: Methods used when you cannot assume your data follows a specific distribution like the Normal curve. 

# Predictive and Multivariate Analysis
These concepts are core to advanced data science and machine learning. 

- Time Series Analysis (ARIMA): Modeling and forecasting patterns in sequential data, like stock prices or weather, by accounting for trends and seasonality.
- Principal Component Analysis (PCA): A dimensionality reduction technique that simplifies complex, high-dimensional datasets while keeping the most important information.
- Multiple and Logistic Regression: Modeling how several independent variables together predict a continuous outcome (Linear) or a categorical one (Logistic).
- Monte Carlo Simulations: Using repeated random sampling to model the behavior of complex systems and estimate various possible outcomes. 

# Distribution Characteristics
- Skewness and Kurtosis: Measuring the asymmetry and "tailedness" (frequency of outliers) of a distribution beyond just its mean and variance.
- Chebyshev's Inequality: A theorem that provides a minimum proportion of data that must fall within a certain number of standard deviations for any distribution.
